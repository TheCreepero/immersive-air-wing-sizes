# Squadron strengths behind the sizes

One air wing stands for one squadron (decided 2026-10-28). Each air sub-unit holds its own size [EQP-008], so one size serves every country and each size is a compromise between the air forces of the period. The figures below are nominal establishment strengths (aircraft on strength, including reserves), as commonly given for 1939 to 1945.

**Status: draft.** The figures were written from general reference knowledge and have not yet been checked against the sources listed at the end. Check each row and add the page or table before the first Workshop release.

| Size | Types | Basis |
|---:|---|---|
| 24 | fighter, carrier fighter, jet fighter, stealth fighter | RAF fighter squadron 16 to 20 (12 flying); USAAF fighter squadron 25 (1943 table); Luftwaffe Jagdstaffel 12 to 16; Soviet fighter squadron 9 to 12 within a regiment of 20 to 40 |
| 18 | heavy fighter, fighter-bomber, close air support, naval bomber, suicide craft | Luftwaffe Stuka and Zerstorer Staffeln 12 to 15; RAF Beaufighter and Typhoon squadrons 16 to 20; US Navy dive and torpedo bomber squadrons 12 to 18 |
| 16 | tactical bomber, jet tactical bomber, medium maritime bomber | RAF Blenheim and Boston squadrons 16 to 18; USAAF medium bomb squadron 12 to 16; Luftwaffe Kampfstaffel 9 to 12 |
| 12 | strategic bomber, jet and intercontinental strategic bomber | USAAF heavy bomb squadron 9 to 12 early, up to 18 later; RAF Bomber Command heavy squadrons 16 to 20 (3-flight squadrons up to 30); Luftwaffe Kampfstaffel 9 to 12 |
| 12 | maritime patrol plane, flying boat, strategic maritime | US Navy patrol squadron (VP) 12; RAF Coastal Command flying boat squadrons 6 to 12 |
| 12 | scout plane, transport plane, rocket interceptor | RAF army co-operation and reconnaissance squadrons 12 to 18; USAAF troop carrier squadron 13; Me 163 Staffel 12 to 16 |

Vanilla gave every type 100, except reconnaissance and patrol planes at 10.

## Sources to check

- Air Ministry and RAF Museum records of squadron establishments.
- United States Army Air Forces tables of organization (the 1943 series for fighter, bomb and troop carrier squadrons), as summarised in Maurer Maurer (ed.), *Air Force Combat Units of World War II*.
- Williamson Murray, *Strategy for Defeat: The Luftwaffe 1933-1945* (unit organisation).
- Von Hardesty, *Red Phoenix* (Soviet regiment and squadron strengths).
- US Navy carrier air group compositions, 1942 (Naval History and Heritage Command).
